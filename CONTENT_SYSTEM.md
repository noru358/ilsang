# CONTENT_SYSTEM

Status: CANONICAL CONTENT AUTHORITY
Updated: 2026-09-09

## Positioning

A short-form Korean daily-life situation comedy for primarily 20–30s readers.

The reader reaction target is closer to:

- “ㅋㅋ 나도 이럼”
- “이거 너잖아”
- “왜 이렇게 현실적이냐”

than to admiration of illustration technique or long narrative immersion.

## Default content pillars

| Pillar | Typical territory |
|---|---|
| Work | commute, meetings, bosses, coworkers, lunch, leaving work |
| Money | payday, card bill, subscriptions, impulse purchases, saving |
| Dating / relationships | texting, dates, ambiguity, introductions, couples |
| Friends | plans, drinking, trips, group chat, personality clashes |
| Living / everyday | cleaning, delivery, laundry, sleep, weekends, errands |

Cross-pillar episodes are encouraged when the situation becomes sharper.

Examples: money × dating, work × relationships, friends × spending.

## Episode unit

One episode should normally center on one concrete situation.

Good:
- “payday confidence disappearing in three days”
- “what your brain does during three hours without a reply”
- “a task arriving five minutes before clock-out”

Weak:
- “my thoughts about work”
- “modern relationships”
- “things that happen in your twenties”

## Six-panel comedy rhythm

Default planning rhythm:

1. HOOK — instantly recognizable premise
2. SETUP — establish the local rule/expectation
3. ESCALATION — pressure or contradiction grows
4. ESCALATION / CONTRAST — a second beat changes the read
5. TURN — choice, reveal, or self-betrayal
6. PAYOFF — shortest convincing punchline or reaction

This is a rhythm target, not six mandatory labels. A strong episode may redistribute beats as long as the six BODY slides read clearly.

## Dialogue rules

Prefer dialogue/reaction that real people might actually say.

Principles:

- short beats over explanatory sentences;
- let visible facts stay visual;
- silence, punctuation, or one-word reactions are valid;
- different scenes should have different conversational rhythms;
- community/thread phrasing may be used when natural;
- do not force memes or slang into every episode.

Avoid “AI explanatory dialogue,” e.g. characters narrating information the panel already shows.

## Comedy engine

Useful engines include:

- expectation → reality
- confident promise → immediate self-betrayal
- three people interpreting the same event differently
- small inconvenience → disproportionate inner reaction
- social politeness → private thought
- optimization attempt → absurdly worse outcome
- everyday rule everyone recognizes but rarely says aloud

Do not hard-code one engine as the project formula.

## Character dependency

The cast is intentionally not defined in this revision.

After the cast redesign is approved, character-specific comic functions may be added as a separate authority document. Until then, storyboards must use neutral role labels such as `PROTAGONIST`, `FRIEND`, `COWORKER`, `DATE`.

## Cover hook

A cover should promise a situation, contradiction, or question—not summarize all six panels.

Prefer concise premise language over a full sentence explaining the plot.

The cover illustration should be distinct from simply copying a BODY cell unless the user explicitly chooses reuse.
